# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People beginning to learn Morse code, returning hobbyists rebuilding recognition speed, and radio-curious learners who want short, focused practice sessions on phones, tablets, or desktop computers. Their primary job is to connect a heard or visible signal to a letter, then build recall through repetition without setup friction.

## Product Purpose

Morse Trainer teaches a focused starter alphabet through one guided path. Learn is a short session in which the trainer, not the learner, chooses each exercise from that learner's own recognition data — a review, a weak character, a contrast pair, or a group of characters that makes a real word. Sprint is an earned checkpoint that opens once enough characters are in play, and Send is the paddle. Nobody has to know what Koch, Farnsworth or adaptive weighting are to benefit from them. The same learning flow can be experienced through three distinct vintage-machine directions—an amber terminal, a broadcast teletext page, and a 1983 pocket trainer—without changing the underlying rules or progress. Success means the learner can start immediately, hear every signal clearly, understand feedback at a glance, and steadily improve accuracy and recall speed.

Where a learner starts is measured, not asked. Instead of offering a beginner a speed dial they have no way to judge, the guide can run a two-minute same-or-different trial — twelve pairs of signals, no Morse knowledge needed — and sets the starting character speed to the fastest rhythm they could still tell apart, with the room between characters coming from how long they took to decide.

The trainer chooses the exercise, and it says why when there is a reason: a session runs in five visible parts — arrive on what is known, repair the weak spots, take delivery of a new character, mix, then use it on real words — and a character that comes back carries one plain clause explaining itself. Progress is not the same as memory, so **stability needs a real gap**: repeating a character inside one sitting proves it is in short-term memory and buys nothing, while the same character answered cleanly across a night, then a week, then a month, is the only thing worth counting. And a new character waits on workload rather than on a count — while reviews are piling up, while more than two characters are still settling, or while accuracy has slipped, the next letter simply does not arrive, and it resumes only once there is real room rather than the moment the learner scrapes past a line. Meaningful messages arrive as soon as their letters are taught: twelve unknown transmissions from stations that are not us sit behind the alphabet, and each one comes through the moment every character inside it has genuinely been learned. The learner never has to read any of this; it shows up as which exercise arrives next, how brightly a character burns in the letters drawer, and one plain sentence at the end of a session. Motivation is stated from evidence and never inferred: every line that could encourage — what came due while they were away, which characters survived a night, how fast they are recognising signals against a week ago, the first time something is held for a week — is derived from the recorded attempt log and is omitted whenever the log does not support it.

## Brand Personality

Focused, tactile, nostalgic. The product should feel like a real-purpose instrument with the small charm of vintage electronics: confident and playful, never childish or theatrical.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards with interchangeable glass cards, gradients, and decorative metrics.
- Childlike gamification that turns practice into a noisy reward loop.
- Retro styling that sacrifices readable type, accessible contrast, or familiar controls for visual novelty.
- A static concept gallery: every direction must remain a fully usable version of the same learning tool.

## Design Principles

- Make the signal the strongest element on every screen.
- Preserve one predictable learning model across all three visual directions.
- Let each machine metaphor shape the controls, typography, and feedback without obscuring function.
- Reward progress with immediate, calm feedback instead of interruption or spectacle.
- Adapt structurally to the device: focused handheld controls on mobile and useful learning context on larger screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard and touch input, visible focus, 44px minimum touch targets, reduced motion, zoom and large text without horizontal scrolling, and status feedback that does not rely on color alone. Morse signals must remain visible as patterns alongside audio so deaf, hard-of-hearing, muted-device, and sound-sensitive learners can use the core experience.
